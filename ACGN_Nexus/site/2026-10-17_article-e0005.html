Abstract

ACGN (anime, comics, games, and novels) culture has profoundly shaped the cognitive frameworks through which an entire generation perceives reality. Beginning with the core player impulse toward "full route completion" and "full collection" in galgame (romance adventure games), this essay draws on modal realism (Lewis, 1986), the psychology of counterfactual thinking (Gilovich & Medvec, 1995), and the Zeigarnik effect (Zeigarnik, 1927) to examine how ACGN narrative structures furnish us with a distinctive "parallel-world intuition"—a heightened awareness of the irreversibility of life choices. Focusing on choice and missed connection in romantic relationships, the essay argues that relationships which never materialized are harder to release than those which ended; that the act of breaking up simultaneously constitutes both avoidance and courage; and ultimately, that the ACGN audience's narrative intuition that "every possibility deserves to be honored" is at once a gift of emotional sensitivity and a cognitive burden unique to this generation.
